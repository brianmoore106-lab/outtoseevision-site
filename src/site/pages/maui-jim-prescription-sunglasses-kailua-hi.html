The Problem With Paradise

Hawaii Is Beautiful. It Is Also Blinding.

Stand on Kailua Beach at noon and look out toward the Mokuluas. The water should be five shades of blue. Instead, a white sheet of light sits on top of it, and you squint until your face hurts.

That white sheet is glare. It is sunlight that has bounced off something flat, like water, sand, a wet road, or the hood of the car in front of you. It carries no detail of its own. All it does is cover up what is underneath.

Ordinary dark lenses do not fix it. They turn everything down at once, the glare and the view together. The world gets dimmer, but it does not get clearer.

Glare Takes the Detail

You cannot see into the water, read the road ahead, or follow the ball.

Glare Takes the Color

Blues go grey. Greens go flat. The island you came for looks washed out.

Glare Takes the Comfort

A day of squinting leaves your eyes tired long before the sun goes down.

The Maui Jim Story

Born on a Maui Beach

Maui Jim started in the 1980s, selling sunglasses on Kaanapali Beach in Lahaina. The people behind it lived under the same sun we do. They wanted a lens that could take the glare off the water without dulling the colors that make Hawaii look like Hawaii.

First they solved the glare. Then they found a way to make color look richer through the lens instead of darker. They called the result PolarizedPlus2®, and it is still at the heart of every pair of Maui Jim sunglasses.

That is why we carry them. Maui Jim was designed for the exact light we live in.

The Lens

What PolarizedPlus2® Actually Does

Every Maui Jim sun lens does three jobs at the same time.

  • It removes the glare. A polarizing film inside the lens blocks the flat, bounced light from water, roads, and sand, so you see what is underneath it.
  • It protects your eyes. The lens blocks 100% of UVA and UVB rays. Maui Jim sunglasses carry The Skin Cancer Foundation Seal of Recommendation.
  • It brings the color back. Maui Jim blends three rare earth elements into the lens to lift reds, greens, and blues. The view looks richer, not just darker.

Here is the test. Put a pair on and look outside. Take them off. Put them back on. That second look tells you everything.

Nine layers, one lens

A Maui Jim lens looks like one piece. It is really a stack of layers, and each one has a job. Layer 1 faces the sun. Layer 9 faces your eye. Tap a layer to see what it does.

  1. The outermost layer. Rain, salt spray, and sunscreen bead up and wipe off instead of smearing.

  2. A mirror applied to the top and bottom of the lens, where the sky above and the ground below are brightest. Maui Jim says it squints for you. On lenses like Blue Hawaii, a color mirror sits here.

  3. CLEARSHELL is Maui Jim's scratch resistant treatment. Here it guards the front of the lens.

  4. Lens material blended with three rare earth elements that lift reds, greens, and blues.

  5. The heart of the lens. This film blocks the flat, bounced light we call glare. Maui Jim says it removes 99.9% of it.

  6. A second layer of color enhancing material, so the polarizing film sits sealed in the middle of the lens.

  7. The same scratch resistant treatment, on the back of the lens.

  8. Stops light that comes from behind you from bouncing off the back of the lens and into your eyes.

  9. The same easy to clean finish, on the side that faces your eye.

This is an illustration, not an exact simulation. Real lenses look better than any screen, so come look through them in the shop. Lens details are from Maui Jim. Lens Colors

Choose Your Lens by Your Day

Maui Jim makes four core lens colors, and each one is tuned for a different kind of light. The right one depends on where you spend your time.

Neutral Grey

Best for bright, direct sun

The darkest Maui Jim lens, with the most light reduction and true to life color. Think beach days, boating, offshore fishing, and long drives at midday.

HCL Bronze

Best for everyday, changing light

HCL stands for High Contrast Lens. A warm tint that adds contrast from full sun to passing clouds. A strong pick for driving, hiking, and all day wear.

Maui Rose

Best for fast moving sports

A soft rose tint with the highest contrast Maui Jim makes, so the ball or the edge of the trail stands out. Think tennis, pickleball, and trail running.

Maui HT

Best for lower light

HT stands for High Transmission. It lets more light in while still adding contrast and color. Made for overcast days, early mornings, and the golf course.

Mirror Finishes

Maui Jim also adds mirror coatings over these colors. Blue Hawaii is a blue mirror on Neutral Grey. Hawaii Lava is a red mirror on HCL Bronze. Maui Sunrise is a pink mirror on Maui Rose. MAUIGreen is a green mirror. A mirror reflects more light away from the lens, and it changes how your sunglasses look from the outside.

Lens Materials

What the Lens Is Made Of Matters Too

Color is half the choice. The material decides how the lens feels on your face and how it holds up to real life.

  • SuperThin Glass. The sharpest optics and the best scratch resistance Maui Jim offers. It is 32% thinner and lighter than standard glass.
  • MauiBrilliant. Optics nearly as clear as glass, at about one third of the weight.
  • MauiPure. Maui Jim's most popular material. Very light, with excellent scratch and shatter resistance.
  • MauiPure LT. A version of MauiPure that is 35% thinner, for the lightest feel on your face.
  • Maui Evolution. A high index material that blends the best traits of glass and polycarbonate. Light, tough, and scratch resistant.

Prescription lenses come in a shorter list of materials, and the best one depends on your prescription and your frame. We will walk you through it at the counter.

The Most Common Mistake

Need a Prescription? Do Not Buy Them Off the Rack.

It sounds like a smart plan. Buy a pair of Maui Jims at a sunglass shop, then take them somewhere to have prescription lenses put in.

But think about what you just paid for. The lens is the reason a Maui Jim costs what it does. The moment your prescription lenses go in, those polarized lenses come out, and they never go back. You paid for Maui Jim lenses you will never look through.

You Pay for Lenses Twice

Once for the polarized lenses that came in the frame, and again for the prescription lenses that replace them.

You May Lose the Maui Jim Lens

Many shops fill the frame with a different brand of lens. It is no longer PolarizedPlus2, so the view will not look the way it did when you fell for the pair.

You Skip the Fitting

Prescription sun lenses work best when someone checks how the frame sits on your face before the lenses are made.

So order the prescription pair from the start. You pay for one set of lenses, Maui Jim makes them, and they are measured for you.
