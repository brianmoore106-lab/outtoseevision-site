How long does a comprehensive eye exam take?

Most comprehensive eye exams take about 45 minutes, depending on what we find and any additional testing you might need. We never rush — if your eyes need extra time, we take it. We reserve a full hour for you.

What's included in a comprehensive eye exam?

A lot more than the basic chart and prescription exam you may be used to. Every comprehensive exam at

OUT TO SEE includes:

• Visual acuity testing (the eye chart)

• Pupil and eye muscle evaluation

• Peripheral (side) vision check

• Digital prescription testing

• Eye pressure measurement (glaucoma screening)

• Microscope evaluation of the front of the eye (eyelids, cornea, iris, lens)

• Optomap widefield retinal imaging — a wide angle photo of the back of your eye that catches

retinal disease, diabetes, hypertension, and more

• OCT scanning of your macula and optic nerve

• Corneal topography measurement

What you won't get: surprise upcharges for "premium" tests, or pressure to skip dilation just to save money.

Everything that's standard of care is included.

Are you a licensed optometrist or an ophthalmologist?

Out to See is a licensed optometry practice. Dr. Lauren Mordue is a Doctor of Optometry (OD), trained at the New England College of Optometry in Boston. She's licensed to perform comprehensive eye exams, diagnose and manage most eye conditions, prescribe glasses and contact lenses, and provide medical eye care for issues like dry eye, eye infections, and red eye exams.

If you have a condition that requires surgery or specialty care (cataract removal, LASIK, retinal surgery), Dr. Mordue will refer you to a trusted ophthalmologist on Oahu and coordinate your care.

Quick reference: an optometrist (OD) handles your day to day eye health, prescriptions, and most medical eye care. An ophthalmologist (MD) is a medical doctor who also performs eye surgery. Most people see an optometrist for routine and even most medical eye care; you will need an ophthalmologist for surgical situations.
